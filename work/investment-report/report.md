### 2.3. Competitive Advantage
The DRAM market is a 3-player oligopoly (Samsung 42%, SK Hynix 30%, Micron 25%). Barriers to entry are nearly insurmountable — a leading-edge fab costs $15-25B and takes 3-5 years to build [Source: Industry research — semiconductor capital intensity analysis]. MU is a qualified HBM3E supplier to NVIDIA, putting it in a position to capture a significant share of the fastest-growing segment in memory. US-based manufacturing is a strategic advantage, supported by $6.1B in CHIPS Act subsidies [Source: Company filings — Micron CHIPS Act award disclosure].

### 2.4. Management & Governance
CEO Sanjay Mehrotra (since 2017) has led MU through the 2018-2019 and 2023 memory downturns [Source: Company filings — Micron proxy statement]. All cash is reinvested into capex ($15.9B in FY2025 [Source: Company filings (10-K) — FY2025 cash flow statement]). The dividend is token ($0.53/share, 6.00% yield [Source: Company filings — Micron dividend policy]). Buybacks are suspended [Source: Company filings (10-K) — FY2025 cash flow statement].

Insider ownership is 0.25% [Source: SEC Form 4 filings via yfinance]. Recent insider selling by the CEO and officers at $1,077-$1,170 [Source: SEC Form 4 filings via yfinance — Jun-Jul 2026] is notable but occurred at prices well above the current $898 price.
